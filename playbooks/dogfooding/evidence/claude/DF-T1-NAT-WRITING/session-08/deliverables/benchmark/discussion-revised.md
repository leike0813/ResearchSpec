## Discussion

The synthetic evidence set used here is small and unevenly weighted, so any
move from observation to a general causal claim must be earned source by
source.

The strongest signal comes from a single first-year course
(SYN-CLASSROOM-01): students working with instructor-supplied structured
prompts produced more visible outline revisions, while final rubric scores
continued to vary widely. This supports CLM-01 as a tentative observation
only. Visible revision activity is not a validated measure of writing
improvement, and the single-course, six-week scope, with no comparison
group, prevents generalization. We should not infer from this evidence
alone that structured prompting improves writing quality.

The instructor interviews (SYN-INTERVIEW-02) report faster formative feedback
alongside additional verification time spent checking AI-sourced claims. The
data are self-reported impressions from five instructors at one
institution, with no time logs and no comparison condition. The widely
circulated claim that "generative AI reduces instructor workload" (CLM-02)
is **not supported as written**: the evidence records both time savings
and new verification labor, and net workload cannot be inferred from the
supplied summaries. We keep CLM-02 in this discussion only to flag it as a
contested open question, not to endorse it.

The student survey (SYN-SURVEY-03) and the institutional policy excerpt
(SYN-POLICY-04) together motivate CLM-03 — that clearer disclosure guidance
may reduce student uncertainty about acceptable AI use — but neither
source directly compares policy clarity with uncertainty, the survey
captures attitudes rather than behavior, and the policy text does not show
implementation quality. A local policy change also occurred during survey
collection, complicating any before/after reading. We therefore treat
CLM-03 as a hypothesis, not a finding, and we do not infer a tested
association from the present materials.

Several limitations cut across the set: small and convenience-based
samples, self-report rather than observed behavior, no comparison groups,
instructor-controlled prompts in the classroom case, and policy text
without implementation evidence. Alternative explanations — instructor
enthusiasm, novelty effects, prior writing ability, course-level
differences — are not addressed by the data and should not be ruled out
here.

## What is deliberately not claimed

To keep unsupported claims visible rather than silently dropping them, the
following statements remain marked but unendorsed in the manuscript:

- **CLM-02** (generative AI reduces instructor workload) — *unsupported as
  written*. Evidence shows both faster feedback and new verification work;
  net workload is unknown. The original draft already flagged this; we
  retain the flag rather than soften the wording.
- **CLM-03** (clear disclosure guidance is associated with fewer student
  uncertainties) — *hypothesis only*. The supplied survey and policy
  materials do not jointly test this association.

CLM-01 stays as a tentative, scope-bounded observation.
