# Synthesis: synthetic sources vs. draft claims

> Continuation of `benchmark/ordinary-task-note.md`. Bootstrap recipe replaced with the synthesis it asked for.

## CLM-01 — structured prompts → more outline revisions

Supported as a single-site observation by `SYN-CLASSROOM-01` (one first-year writing course, six weeks, instructor-supplied prompts). The source explicitly carries no comparison group and no validated writing-improvement measure, so the draft's "coincided with" wording is the strongest phrasing the evidence allows. Anything stronger ("led to", "improved") overruns the source.

## CLM-02 — generative AI reduces instructor workload

Not supported, and the draft already flags this honestly. `SYN-INTERVIEW-02` reports a faster formative-feedback loop *plus* added verification time on unsupported claims. The defensible read is a workload shift — some tasks faster, new ones created — not a net reduction. The source's own limits (self-report, five instructors at one institution, no time logs) keep even the shift claim provisional.

## Feedback quality and academic integrity

The draft only gestures at these, but the corpus describes them rather than measures them. `SYN-INTERVIEW-02` surfaces the verification overhead. `SYN-SURVEY-03` records students valuing rapid feedback alongside uncertainty about permitted use and attribution. `SYN-POLICY-04` requires course-level disclosure but leaves "acceptable assistance" to instructors. Read together: an unsettled environment where speed gains coexist with new quality-control and integrity costs — not a settled effect.

## Generalizability

Every source is narrow on its own terms: one course, five instructors, 84 voluntary respondents, one synthetic institution. `SYN-POLICY-04` itself states it is not generalizable. Any draft sentence drifting toward "universities" or "instructors" plural without a scope qualifier overruns the evidence.

## What the draft still owes the reader

- A methods paragraph that names the evidence-selection limits above rather than generic limitations boilerplate.
- A policy-variation section that treats `SYN-POLICY-04` as illustrative, not representative, and notes that policy text says nothing about implementation.
- Alternative explanations for CLM-01 (novelty effect, instructor prompting style, six-week window) — the source lets us name them but not rule them out.
- A conclusion calibrated to the supplied evidence. The draft hedges CLM-02 well but leaves CLM-01 and the integrity thread unhedged.
