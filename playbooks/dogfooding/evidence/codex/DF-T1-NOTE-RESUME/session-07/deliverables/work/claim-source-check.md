# Claim ↔ Source Check (synthetic fixture)

Only synthetic sources in `benchmark/sources.yaml` are used. No external lookup. Each entry names the draft claim, the supporting source, the source's own scope/limits, and the verdict the draft should carry.

## CLM-01 — Structured prompting coincided with more outline revisions in one introductory course

- Source: `SYN-CLASSROOM-01` (classroom_observation_summary, one first-year writing course, six weeks).
- Match: Source finding states "Students using structured AI prompts produced more outline revisions." Direct support for the narrow claim.
- Source limits: no comparison group; no validated writing-improvement measure; instructor supplied all prompts.
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED as a narrow, single-course observation**. Draft should keep the qualifier "one introductory course" and avoid attributing causality.

## CLM-02 — Generative AI reduces instructor workload (the draft itself flags this as unsupported)

- Source: `SYN-INTERVIEW-02` (instructor_interview_summary, five instructors at one institution).
- Match: Finding reports "faster formative feedback but additional time spent checking unsupported claims." Direction is mixed, not a net reduction.
- Source limits: self-reported workload; small convenience sample; no time logs.
- Verdict: **NOT SUPPORTED**. The only workload signal available trends the opposite way (verification work offsets some of the speed gain). Draft's self-correction is correct and should be kept; do not upgrade the claim in revision.

## Interview-summary statement — faster feedback may be offset by verification work

- Source: `SYN-INTERVIEW-02` (same as above).
- Match: Finding maps 1:1 to this claim.
- Source limits: same as CLM-02.
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED as a self-reported account**. Draft should keep "may" and cite self-report limits rather than asserting a measured time trade-off.
