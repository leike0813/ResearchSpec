## Scope and evidence limits

Four sources cover this study: one six-week classroom observation, five instructor interviews, an 84-respondent student survey, and one institutional policy excerpt. None include a comparison group, time logs, or behavioral measures. The discussion stays within these bounds and does not generalize beyond them.

## Revision activity, not revision quality

SYN-CLASSROOM-01 supports CLM-01 only as tentative. Structured prompting coincided with more outline revisions, but final rubric scores varied widely and no validated writing-quality measure was applied. Revision activity is observable; revision quality is not. Instructor-designed prompts and rubric inconsistency are plausible alternative explanations that the data do not rule out, so CLM-01 should be reported with that ceiling rather than as an effect of "AI use" in general.

## Workload as a tradeoff, not a reduction

SYN-INTERVIEW-02 reports faster formative feedback alongside new verification work. CLM-02 — that generative AI reduces instructor workload — is unsupported_as_written in the supplied evidence. The supported characterization is a tradeoff: time saved on initial feedback is partly offset by time spent checking AI-assisted claims. The interview sample is small, self-reported, and not paired with time logs. Staffing or policy decisions that treat workload reduction as established should wait for measured studies.

## Policy variation and student uncertainty

SYN-POLICY-04 requires course-level disclosure but leaves the definition of acceptable assistance to individual instructors. SYN-SURVEY-03 records some respondents uncertain about permitted use and attribution. CLM-03 — that clear disclosure guidance is associated with fewer student uncertainties — is hypothesis_only: the sources describe policy text and student attitudes separately and do not directly link policy clarity to reported uncertainty. The hypothesis is plausible but currently exceeds the evidence, and any institutional claim that "clear policy reduces uncertainty" should be marked as untested.

## Alternative explanations kept in view

Three non-AI explanations remain live for the patterns above and were not ruled out by the supplied materials:
- The revision pattern may reflect prompt specificity or instructor modeling rather than AI use per se.
- The instructor-reported tradeoff may be a novelty effect that fades with routine adoption.
- Student uncertainty may reflect policy ambiguity, broader academic-integrity messaging, or voluntary-response bias rather than AI exposure itself.

## Conclusion, calibrated to the evidence

Across the four supplied sources, the most defensible statement is that structured prompting coincided with more visible revision activity in one introductory course (CLM-01, tentative). Workload reduction (CLM-02, unsupported_as_written) and the policy-clarity effect on uncertainty (CLM-03, hypothesis_only) should remain flagged rather than promoted to findings. Three follow-ups would tighten the evidence base: a comparison-group classroom study with a validated writing-quality measure; instructor time logs that capture both feedback and verification work; and a survey or interview design that links policy clarity to student-reported uncertainty directly.
