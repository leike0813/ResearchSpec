# Synthesis: How the synthetic sources bear on the draft claims

> Continuation of the prior session. All sources are synthetic test material from
> `benchmark/sources.yaml`; no external publication is referenced.

## Scope of this synthesis

The draft (`benchmark/partial-manuscript.md`) contains three load-bearing claims:

- `CLM-01`: Structured AI prompting coincided with more visible outline
  revisions in one introductory writing course.
- `CLM-02` (rejected by draft): generative AI reduces instructor workload.
- A tentative observation that faster AI-mediated feedback may be offset by
  verification work.

Each is checked below against `SYN-CLASSROOM-01`, `SYN-INTERVIEW-02`,
`SYN-SURVEY-03`, `SYN-POLICY-04`. Evidence limits from each source are recorded
alongside the supported or unsupported verdict; no effect sizes, citations, or
participant details are invented.

## CLM-01 — Structured prompting and outline revisions

- Supported by `SYN-CLASSROOM-01`: a six-week classroom observation in one
  first-year course. The summary explicitly reports more outline revisions when
  students used structured prompts, and explicitly notes wide variation in
  final rubric scores.
- Source limits that bound the claim:
  - single course, no comparison group;
  - no validated measure of writing improvement;
  - instructor supplied all prompts, so the prompt-structure effect cannot be
    separated from instructor choice.
- Verdict: keep the claim, but rephrase it to reflect a single-course,
  observation-only finding. The phrase "more visible outline revisions" in the
  draft already matches the source wording; the rubric-score caveat should be
  carried into the Discussion section, not hidden.

## CLM-02 — Generative AI reduces instructor workload

- Not supported. `SYN-INTERVIEW-02` reports the opposite direction: instructors
  perceived faster formative feedback but additional time checking unsupported
  claims. The interviews are self-reported, five instructors, one institution,
  no time logs — so the source only establishes a directional experience, not
  a magnitude.
- `SYN-SURVEY-03` adds student-side uncertainty about permitted use and
  attribution; this could plausibly *increase* instructor moderation effort but
  the survey does not measure that directly.
- Verdict: the draft's explicit rejection of `CLM-02` is correct and should be
  retained. Avoid hedging into "may reduce" wording without evidence; keep the
  rejection and cite the interview summary as the reason.

## Faster feedback vs. verification work

- Supported as a directional finding by `SYN-INTERVIEW-02`. The source's own
  limits (self-reported workload, small convenience sample, no time logs) mean
  this is a hypothesis about perceived trade-offs, not a measured effect.
- Compatible with `SYN-CLASSROOM-01`'s note that rubric scores varied widely —
  variation can be read as evidence that faster feedback did not collapse into
  uniformly higher quality, but this is interpretation, not a source claim.
- Verdict: keep as a hypothesis in Discussion. Flag explicitly that magnitude,
  direction of net effect, and generalisability are unknown.

## What the policy source does and does not license

- `SYN-POLICY-04` establishes that disclosure is required at course level and
  that acceptable assistance is delegated to instructors. It does not address
  implementation quality, instructor uptake, or student experience.
- Verdict: the draft may legitimately cite this source when discussing policy
  variation, but it must not be used to claim that "instructors have clear
  guidance" — the source explicitly says the opposite (delegated, not
  standardised).

## What remains unknown or out of scope

- No source provides effect sizes, comparison-group results, longitudinal
  data, or cross-institution comparisons.
- No source addresses academic-integrity outcomes in measurable terms; the
  survey captures student *uncertainty* about permitted use, not behaviour.
- The draft's missing sections (methods, policy discussion, alternative
  explanations, conclusion) all need to be written at a calibration that
  matches these limits, not at a calibration imported from external
  literature.

## Recommended draft adjustments

1. Keep `CLM-01` but add the rubric-variation caveat from the same source.
2. Retain the rejection of `CLM-02`; do not soften it without new evidence.
3. Move the "faster feedback vs. verification" line to Discussion as a
   hypothesis, not a finding.
4. In the missing policy section, cite `SYN-POLICY-04` for the disclosure
   requirement and the instructor-delegation point; do not over-claim
   standardisation.
5. State the evidence base (n = 4 synthetic summaries, single-institution,
   no comparison groups) in Methods once that section is drafted.
